The Last Resort

Scholastic

Role: Product Coordinator · Stack: AR, ML, mobile web

Scholastic wanted to create an interactive experience tied to an upcoming middle grade novel — but it was a new initiative with no existing budget, no vendor, and no defined scope. I came in before anything was built.

I led the brainstorm process and built the pitch deck my manager used to secure the project internally. From there I owned the RFP process end-to-end — researching vendors, drafting requirements, and making the final recommendation (which was selected). I built scope and budget variations for stakeholders at different investment levels and produced internal presentation materials throughout.

The harder problem was the product itself. We had three constraints that shaped every decision: a tight budget, a requirement that the experience tie closely to the book narrative, and strict COPPA compliance that made standard AI approaches a liability. On the AI question specifically, we made a deliberate call to use an ML model that detected input language and returned scripted responses rather than a generative model — keeping the experience safe, on-brand, and out of regulatory and PR risk.

To make the AR puzzles work narratively, I sat in manuscript reviews with the editorial team and flagged moments where the story could be developed to support the interactive layer — character backstories, puzzle logic, moments of reveal. Some of those suggestions made it into the final book.

The Last Resort debuted at #6 on the NYT Middle Grade Bestsellers list. The sequel is in development, still built on the methodology, research, and vendor I recommended.

Slack AI Agent

Pretty Big Monster

Role: Builder and Designer · Stack: n8n, Tavily, Claude API

Designers at PBM needed fast answers on AR and effects feasibility during client pitches. The old process meant messaging a developer, scheduling a call, or passing a deck with questions and waiting. It pulled developers into conversations too early and slowed down the pitch process at the moments that mattered most.

I started by thinking about this as a Notion doc — a reference resource designers could consult. Then a web app. But the real insight was simpler: the best ideas at PBM happen in Slack. That's where the work lives. So I built there instead.

The bot parses live documentation from Snap Lens Studio and TikTok Effect House and returns feasibility assessments directly in Slack, in the flow of the conversation where the question came up. I'd never worked in an agent builder before — figuring out n8n, wiring the APIs together, and configuring the Slack listener events and permissions was genuinely new territory that I navigated by outlining the architecture first and building iteratively.

I presented the finished tool with a full pitch deck to my manager, agency partners, and the dev team. The reception led to two outcomes: buy-in to deploy it on the company Slack (currently in network safety review), and the launch of an internal AI Strike Force where the team now experiments with AI tools and workflows on an ongoing basis. My manager is using the scaffolding as the foundation for a broader internal AI tool for the agency.

Guardians of the Grocery

Parsons Creative Hackathon

Role: Project Manager and Developer · Stack: Next.js, Supabase, ESP32-CAM, Claude Code

The prompt was open-ended. We chose to build something that felt genuinely fun and community-driven, drawing on games we loved as reference points. The product: a web app where players hunt NYC bodega cats and collect them as digital trading cards, with cat data captured wirelessly via a custom ESP32-CAM hardware controller.

The decisions that won were about scope and integration. Rather than designing each trading card individually, we built a system where SVGs auto-populated from backend data — simple on the surface, but it required real architectural thinking to execute. We also prioritized a fully working demo over surface-level polish: functional login, cat data tied to user accounts, a live-populated NYC map, and a hardware controller that spoke wirelessly to the database in real time.

Not everything was shippable — but everything we showed worked, and the core experience held together as a system. That tradeoff was deliberate.

Won Best Software at the Parsons Creative Hackathon.
